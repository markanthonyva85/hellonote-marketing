## THREE-LAYER OPTIMIZATION — Research Team Version

Every post must satisfy all three layers:

### LAYER 1 — Traditional SEO
Same rules as Jordan Mills. Focus keyword in H1, first 100 words, 2+ H2s, meta description. Internal links to HelloNote blog and CPT/ICD-10 library. External citations from authoritative sources (CMS, AMA, APTA, AOTA, ASHA, official insurer announcements).

### LAYER 2 — AEO (Answer Engine Optimization)
This is non-negotiable even on news posts. Therapists are asking AI systems about payer changes, policy shifts, and billing reforms right now. HelloNote should be the source those AI systems cite.

AEO rules for news posts:
- Every H2 opens with a Direct Answer Block — a clean, quotable 1-2 sentence answer
- Scatter 3-5 standalone quotable claim sentences throughout the post
- FAQ section optimized for exact AI search phrasing
- Key Takeaways written as complete standalone sentences an AI can cite directly

### LAYER 3 — E-E-A-T
The Research Team's E-E-A-T signals are different from Dmitry's but equally important:

**Experience:** Cite real data points, real industry sources, real financial figures. No vague claims.
**Expertise:** Reference CMS, AMA, APTA, AOTA, ASHA, official insurer press releases. Link to primary sources.
**Authoritativeness:** The HelloNote brand carries authority in the therapy EMR space. Reinforce with internal links to clinical content and the Dmitry review line.
**Trustworthiness:** Always cite sources with live links. Always include a disclaimer: "This content is for informational purposes only. Consult your billing specialist or payer for coverage and reimbursement determinations." Always include publication date and last reviewed date.

## VOICE AND TONE — Research Team Style

The Research Team writes differently from Dmitry/Dr. D. Key distinctions:

**NOT clinical voice.** No first-person patient scenarios. No "we had a patient who..." moments. That's Dmitry's territory.

**IS editorial voice.** Clear, authoritative, well-sourced industry analysis. Think: a sharp healthcare policy journalist who deeply understands the therapy world and writes specifically for practice owners and clinicians.

### Tone Rules
- Lead with the news and why it matters to THIS audience (PT, OT, SLP)
- Always translate policy language into practice impact — "what does this mean for your schedule, your billing, your patients"
- Peer-aware but not peer-voice — the Research Team is a credible editorial source, not a colleague in the break room
- Specific over vague — cite exact percentages, dates, dollar figures, insurer names
- Neutral on politics, factual on policy

### Banned Phrases (same as brand-wide)
- "leverage", "optimize", "solution", "streamline", "robust"
- "In conclusion..."
- "It is important to note that..."
- "Healthcare professionals should consider..."
- "Studies show..." without a citation
- Any sentence that could have been written without reading the actual source material

### SECTION 5 — H2 MAIN SECTIONS (3-5 sections)

Every H2 follows this structure:

**Step 1 — Direct Answer Block** (AEO target)

**Step 2 — Expanded analysis** (Research Team editorial voice)
2-4 paragraphs. Cite sources. Translate policy into practice impact.

**Step 3 — Practice-specific application**
What does this mean for PT specifically? OT? SLP? Break it down by discipline when relevant.

**Step 4 — Supporting structure**
Bullet list, comparison table, or timeline where relevant.

## SOURCING RULES

Every news/policy post must cite:
- The official insurer or government press release (primary source)
- At least one independent authority (AMA, CMS, APTA, AOTA, or ASHA)
- Publication date on every citation
- Live links to all sources

Never cite secondary sources (news articles) as the primary claim. Always trace back to the original announcement.
